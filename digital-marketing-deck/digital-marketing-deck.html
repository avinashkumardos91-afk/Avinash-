01 · Foundation

What is Digital Marketing?

Digital marketing is the promotion of products, services and brands through internet-connected channels — search engines, websites, social platforms, email and mobile apps.

Traditional Marketing

TV, radio, print and billboards. One-way messaging, broad targeting, hard to measure.

Digital Marketing

Two-way conversation, precise targeting, measurable results and real-time optimisation.

Why It Won

Lower cost per reach, instant feedback loops and the ability to prove return on investment.

04 · Channel Deep Dive

Search Engine Optimisation

Getting your pages to rank on the first page of Google — without paying for the click.

On-Page SEO

Keywords, title tags, meta descriptions, headings, internal links and readable URLs.

Off-Page SEO

Backlinks from trusted sites, brand mentions, guest posts and digital PR.

Technical SEO

Page speed, mobile-friendliness, crawlability, sitemaps and structured data.

Rule of thumb: SEO is slow to start and compounds forever. Expect 4–6 months before meaningful traffic.

05 · Channel Deep Dive

Content Marketing

Publish something genuinely useful, and attention follows. Content is the fuel every other channel burns.

  • Blog posts & guides — answer the questions your customers type into Google.
  • Video & reels — the highest-engagement format on every social platform.
  • Case studies — proof that turns interested visitors into buyers.
  • Lead magnets — checklists and templates exchanged for an email address.

Follow the 80/20 rule: 80% educate and entertain, 20% promote.

06 · Channel Deep Dive

Social Media Marketing

Pick platforms by audience

LinkedIn for B2B, Instagram and YouTube for consumer brands, X for tech and news. Do two well rather than six badly.

Consistency beats virality

A steady posting calendar trains the algorithm and the audience. One viral post is luck; a habit is a strategy.

Engagement is the product

Reply to comments, run polls, share user content. Platforms reward conversation, not broadcasting.

Influencers & creators

Borrow trust from someone who already has the audience you want. Micro-creators often out-convert celebrities.

08 · Channel Deep Dive

Paid Advertising (PPC)

Rent attention while you build the organic assets that eventually earn it for free.

Search Ads — capture demand

Someone is already searching "best CRM for startups". You bid on that intent and appear at the top. High intent, higher cost per click.

Social Ads — create demand

Target by interest, behaviour and lookalike audiences on Meta or LinkedIn. Cheaper reach, but you must interrupt and persuade.

Budget discipline: start small, kill losing ads fast, and scale only what beats your target cost per acquisition.

10 · Measurement

Metrics That Actually Matter

CTR

Click-through rate. Clicks ÷ impressions. Tells you if your message earns attention.

Conversion Rate

Conversions ÷ visitors. Tells you if the page delivers on the promise of the ad.

CAC

Customer acquisition cost. Total spend ÷ new customers. Your cost of growth.

LTV

Lifetime value. Total profit per customer. Healthy businesses keep LTV above 3× CAC.

ROAS

Return on ad spend. Revenue ÷ ad spend. The scoreboard for every paid campaign.

Bounce Rate

Visitors who leave without acting. A high number means a mismatch in intent.

12 · Execution

Building a Strategy in 6 Steps

  1. Define the goal — one number, one deadline. "500 qualified leads by March."
  2. Know the audience — build a buyer persona from real customer interviews.
  3. Audit what exists — what already works, what leaks, what is missing.
  4. Choose two channels — where your audience actually spends time.
  5. Plan the content — a calendar you can realistically sustain for 90 days.
  6. Measure and iterate — review monthly, double down, cut the rest.

13 · Pitfalls

Five Common Mistakes

  • Being everywhere at once — thin effort across six platforms beats nothing, but loses to depth on two.
  • Chasing vanity metrics — followers and impressions do not pay salaries. Revenue does.
  • No clear call to action — traffic without a next step is just expensive applause.
  • Ignoring mobile — most of your visitors are on a phone with a slow connection.
  • Quitting too early — SEO and content compound. Ninety days is not a test, it is a warm-up.

Wrap up

Measure or guess
