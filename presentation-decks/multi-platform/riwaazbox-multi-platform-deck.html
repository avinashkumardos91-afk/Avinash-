01 · The idea

Four platforms, four different jobs

Posting the same thing in four places is not a strategy — it is four times the work for one result. Each channel gets a single job it is best at.

  • Instagram creates the want — unboxing, texture, ribbon, reveal. Nobody searches for a hamper they have not seen yet.
  • YouTube answers the search — "gift hamper for anniversary", "corporate diwali gift ideas". Intent already exists; be the result.
  • LinkedIn opens the bulk order — one HR manager is worth two hundred retail carts.
  • WhatsApp closes it — catalogue, price, payment link, dispatch photo. This is where money actually moves.

The rule: nothing gets posted anywhere until you can say which of these four jobs it is doing.

04 · Instagram

The 30-Second Reel Formula

  1. 0–3s · Hook — one line of text on screen and one striking frame. "Plain gift? Riwaazbox it."
  2. 3–10s · Promise — show the finished box early. People stay for a payoff they can already picture.
  3. 10–22s · Process — fast cuts of the build: box, filler, product, ribbon, seal.
  4. 22–27s · Payoff — the hero shot, held still for a beat. This is the frame that gets screenshotted.
  5. 27–30s · Ask — one CTA only: "DM hamper for the price list."

Caption rule: first line is a hook, second line is the offer, third line is the CTA. Hashtags go below the fold — five relevant beats thirty generic.

05 · YouTube & Shorts

Two Lanes, One Channel

Shorts — reach

Your Instagram reels, re-exported without the Instagram watermark. Same edit, new audience, near-zero extra work. Three a week keeps the channel warm.

Long-form — intent

2–4 minute gift guides shot once a fortnight: "5 corporate hampers under ₹1,000", "How we pack a wedding hamper". These keep earning views months later.

Why bother with long-form: a reel dies in 48 hours. A search-titled video is still bringing in enquiries next Diwali.

05 · YouTube & Shorts

Title It Like a Search Box

Write the title as the sentence a buyer would actually type — then make the video deliver exactly that.

  • "Corporate Diwali gift hampers under ₹1,000" — price and occasion, the two things buyers filter on.
  • "How to pack a wedding gift hamper (step by step)" — a how-to that ends with your product doing the work.
  • "Cheap gift box vs premium hamper — what's the difference?" — comparison searches convert hardest.
  • "Anniversary gift ideas for last-minute shoppers" — urgency built into the query.

Pin a comment with the WhatsApp link on every video. It is the shortest path from a viewer to an order.

06 · LinkedIn

The Bulk-Order Channel

Retail buys one box. Corporate buys two hundred. LinkedIn is the only place the second buyer is reachable by name.

Who to reach

HR and admin leads, office managers, startup founders, event and wedding planners.

What to post

Finished corporate orders, branded-ribbon customisation, dispatch scale, delivery timelines.

What to offer

A one-page rate card: slab pricing, branding options, lead time, delivery cities.

Tone shift: Instagram sells beauty, LinkedIn sells reliability. Same hamper, different sentence — "500 boxes, branded, delivered in six working days."

06 · LinkedIn

A Two-Post Week

  1. Tuesday · proof — a photo of a real order that shipped, with the numbers: quantity, customisation, turnaround.
  2. Thursday · useful — a short piece a gifting buyer can use: budget bands, what goes wrong with cheap packaging, festival lead times.
  3. Every day · ten minutes — comment on posts by HR and admin people in your delivery cities. Visibility here is cheaper than content.
  4. Monthly · the ask — one direct post inviting corporate enquiries, with the rate card offered by DM.

Keep enquiries in one place. A LinkedIn DM that never reaches your order sheet is a lost order.

07 · WhatsApp

Where the Order Actually Closes

  1. Catalogue — every hamper as a product entry with photo, contents and price. No PDF attachments.
  2. Quick replies — saved answers for price, delivery time, customisation and bulk slabs. Reply in seconds, not hours.
  3. Quote — one message with items, total and delivery date. Nothing left for the buyer to calculate.
  4. Payment link — sent in the same thread, so the buyer never leaves the conversation.
  5. Dispatch photo — a picture of their actual packed box. This is the message people forward to friends.

Permission first: broadcast only to people who messaged you or ticked opt-in at checkout. Unsolicited blasts get the number blocked, and the number is the business.

08 · Web

What the Link in Bio Must Do

One page, loaded on a phone, in under three seconds. Everything else is decoration.

  • Hampers with prices — photo, contents, price, in that order. A price hidden behind "enquire" loses the retail buyer.
  • A WhatsApp button on every hamper — pre-filled with the hamper name so you know what they are asking about.
  • A corporate enquiry form — name, company, quantity, date needed. Four fields, no more.
  • Delivery cities and lead time — stated plainly. Half of all questions are this one.
  • A festive banner you actually change — the fastest signal that the business is live and taking orders.

10 · Measurement

Savesthe signal that a hamper is wanted, not just watched

Set your own baseline: these are the metrics to track, not benchmarks to hit. Record four weeks first, then set targets against your own numbers. Follower count is not on this list on purpose.

11 · Rollout

The First 90 Days

  1. Weeks 1–4 · Foundation — WhatsApp catalogue live, landing page with prices, LinkedIn page filled in, one shoot done. Publish on Instagram only.
  2. Weeks 5–8 · Volume — add YouTube Shorts from existing edits and the two LinkedIn posts a week. Nothing new to shoot, only to re-cut.
  3. Weeks 9–12 · Festive push — long-form gift guides, corporate rate card in circulation, weekly WhatsApp broadcast to the opted-in list.
  4. Ongoing · Review — one hour a month: which pillar earned the DMs, which platform earned the orders. Cut whatever earned neither.

Wrap up

Three Things to Remember

One job each

Instagram wants, YouTube searches, LinkedIn buys in bulk, WhatsApp closes.

Shoot once

One table, one afternoon, a fortnight of posts across four platforms.

Count conversations

Messages started, not followers gained. Everything upstream serves that number.

Gift better. Gift beautifully.
